A tiny vision module that fires while the slow VLA is still thinking — and decides, on its own, when to interrupt the plan.

§ Manifesto

Modern Vision-Language-Action models can read a kitchen, parse an instruction, and decide how to grasp a mug. They cannot do all of this fast.

On consumer GPUs a single chunk costs ~100 ms. The arm then executes a half-second action sequence open-loop. A nudge, a slipped grip, an object that moves — all are invisible until the next chunk lands.

The remedy is not a bigger VLA. It is a smaller companion that watches the wrist camera every step, asks: does this need a correction, or do I trust the plan? — and acts before the slow thinker can reply.

§ 01
Folio · The Pain Point

VLAs are too large to close the loop.

An action chunk is a forecast. The robot rolls it out for half a second, and during that interval it is, in the strict sense, deaf to the world. Should the cup nudge two centimetres, should the peg's edge catch the rim, the policy will discover the mistake only at the next inference — by which time the trajectory has already drifted into failure.

The naive instinct is to make the VLA faster. But foundation-scale models hit their floor at the limits of the silicon. The instinct that actually works is older than reinforcement learning: separate the two clocks. Let the planner think slowly. Let a small, dedicated organ watch the wrist and react.

100 ms thinking, 400 ms blind. The chunk is a contract the robot cannot revise. § Observation, May 2026

Folio · The System Atlas

Two clocks, one robot.

Above the line, the slow Vision-Language-Action model deliberates in chunks. Below it, a wrist-camera companion runs at every control tick — semantically tethered to the slow VLA through two detached embeddings, structurally cut off from everything else.

§ 05
Folio · The Merge

Where two loops meet.

The gate is the moral organ of the system. It decides whether to trust the slow plan or to nudge it. Crucially, that decision is learned — not magnitude-detected.

δa_clip = clamp(δa, −δ_max, +δ_max) a_cand = a_base + g · δa_clip a_final = SafetyLayer(a_cand, prev_a, dt)
A gate is not a clip. Clipping limits how loud you can correct. A gate decides whether you should correct at all. § Architecture note
§ 06
Folio · The Three Losses

Three signals, one controller.

L · DELTA

Supervise the residual.

L_δ = ‖ δa − (a_expert − a_base) ‖²

Offline: roll the frozen SmolVLA over each LIBERO demo to compute a_base per step. The expert action supplies a_expert.

L · GATE (G-C)

Supervise trust, not magnitude.

δ̄a = stop_grad(δa) g* = σ( ‖a_e−a_b‖² − ‖a_e−(a_b+δ̄a)‖² ) L_g = BCE(g, g*)

If the correction strictly reduces error, the gate target is one; if not, zero. Stop-gradient breaks the chicken-and-egg.

L · CONTACT

An auxiliary teacher.

L_c = BCE(c, contact_label)

From MuJoCo contact tensor in simulation; from joint-torque heuristic on the real arm. Used in training only — never at inference.

L_total = L_δ + 1.0 · L_g + 0.1 · L_c
§ 07
Folio · The Edges

What this paper refuses to be.

A small contribution earns its keep by being honest about what it does not promise. The following lie outside the scope of this draft.

  • This is not a long-horizon re-planner or adaptive-horizon study.
  • This is not a world model. Expected-state prediction is deferred.
  • This is not a force/tactile fusion paper. Vision is the only sense.
  • This is not a LoRA comparison. Our claim is about inference-time reactivity, not parameter count.
  • This is not a new VLA. The base policy is SmolVLA, untouched.
